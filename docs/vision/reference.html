myGFO Vision Reference

The working detail behind the vision: what exists today, the plan and its gates, the designs, every idea and every decision.

For the myGFO committee and the developer. The short version for leadership is the myGFO Vision and Roadmap story page. Written by Bryan Dunlop with Claude. It is not the developer's work plan.

50 ideas tracked 20 in the roadmap 5 to discuss 4 stay in other apps 21 not discussed yet

Vision · 1

Manifesto

A corporate flight department is a dozen specialist teams passing one thing between them: the trip, and the airplane that flies it. Today that hand-off runs on retyping, email, phone calls and memory. The same passenger is entered three times. A schedule change is noticed by whoever happens to look. A revised manual is published and nobody can say who has read it.

The work gets done because good people carry it in their heads. That does not survive a growing fleet, and it does not survive turnover.

1

Enter once, flow everywhere

A fact has one home. The allergy an EA enters is the allergy the flight attendant reads, the caterer receives and the trip sheet prints.

2

Tell people what they need, when they need it

Notifications come from the state of the world, not from whoever remembered to send one. When the condition clears, the alert clears itself.

3

Silence is never consent

When a signature, status or checklist item is missing, the system asks, asks again, then escalates. Urgent problems go to whoever is on call. Overdue work goes to the manager or their delegate.

4

Documents are alive

Important changes go out at once as bulletins that point at the section they amend. The next revision absorbs them and the bulletins retire. Readers send suggestions straight to the owner.

5

Every hand-off is a conversation with a record

The Tech Log is the model. A pilot and a mechanic talk about a broken airplane, and the conversation is the signed, unchangeable record.

6

Systems of record stay where they belong

CAMP owns airworthiness, myairops the schedule, Workday people and ForeFlight the cockpit. myGFO connects them, and is the record only at the moment of its own signature.

7

Safety rules are code

MEL clocks, A&P certificate checks and RII separation are enforced and tested by the software, not left to training.

8

Machines prepare, humans decide

Automation and AI gather, check, draft and warn. A pilot decides whether the airplane can depart that runway today.

9

Privacy and security are default-deny

Who is on a trip and where they are goes to the smallest audience for the shortest time. Passengers hear from us by email first, and get an app only if they ask.

10

Fewer apps, not more

Crew live in myGFO and ForeFlight. Scheduling and EAs live in myGFO and myairops. myGFO pushes into the tools people already use, including Outlook and Teams.

11

Knowledge compounds

Every trip should leave the department knowing more. A short debrief, reviewed before it becomes guidance, carries what one crew learned to the next.

12

Every screen answers one question

The hangar wall, the scheduling wall, the lead's view and the EA's home each answer the question that room asks most often, without a click.

What myGFO is not

  • Not a maintenance tracking system. CAMP is.
  • Not the scheduling system of record. myairops is, and myGFO only reads from it.
  • Not a flight planner or a performance calculator. ForeFlight and the AFM are.
  • Not an HR system. Workday is.
  • Not the home of crew legality, training records or flight data monitoring. Those stay in the apps that do them today.
  • Not a surveillance tool. Accountability is about the work, not about watching people.

Plan · 3

The shared platform

Every feature rests on the same eight pieces. Building them per module is how the prototype got broad. Building them once is how production gets connected.

1Identity and people

Entra sign-in, one record per human, roles, the manager chain and named delegates. Joiners, movers and leavers start here. People outside P&G need their own path in.

2Trip graph

One trip with its legs, aircraft, crew and passengers, keyed to myairops. Checklists, briefings, catering, documents and tax hang off it.

3Events and notifications

Changes become events that re-fire checklist items and notify each audience. Two ladders: urgent goes to on-call, overdue goes to the manager or delegate. Quiet hours and crew rest are handled here, once. Channels include push, email, Teams and Outlook.

4Expiry engine

Everything that expires and who must act: crew and passenger documents, aircraft documents, company airport pages, document reviews, and training dates read from myairops.

5Integration hub

One adapter per outside system behind a capability gate: myairops, CAMP, ForeFlight, FlightAware, Outlook, Workday, Jeppesen and Airflow. Vendor shapes and units stop at the boundary.

6Signature and audit

One e-signature service with RFC 8785 hashing, append-only ledger tables and server re-stamping. Used by the Tech Log, document acknowledgments and crew acceptance.

7Insight data layer

A read-only analytical copy. Dashboards, wall screens, tax and agents read from here, never from production tables.

8Agent gateway

myGFO's tools for AI agents, as separate MCP servers by sensitivity, acting on behalf of the signed-in person. Read first, drafts later, never commits.

Plan · 4

Horizons

A horizon starts when its gates clear, not when a calendar says so. Dates go in only once the developer's capacity is known.

H0

Land what we are building

Document Center and Tech Log run in production, used by real people.

  • Confirm the platform minimum: Entra, Azure SQL ledger tables, Key Vault, the signature service and the notification core.
  • Build the two escalation ladders and delegates into the notification core from the start.
  • Document Center: finish the living-document model, mirror the pilot content pack to ForeFlight Documents, reconcile the group drives, and escalate unsigned reading to the manager.
  • Tech Log: production backend, offline iPad build and the CAMP sandbox read path. Settle the DOM rulings before the ledger schema freezes, because ledger schema cannot be reverted.
  • Write the Tech Log's paper fallback procedure. Phone and email cover scheduling, but in the Tech Log the signature is the record.
  • Every mockup names its gate, so nobody expects what an open question still blocks.

GatesBoth modules used on real trips for a month. Immutability, idempotency and hash tests green. A CAMP sandbox round trip proven.

H1

A pilot or flight attendant opens one place and sees everything about the next trip, and ForeFlight already has it.

  • The trip graph, pulled from myairops.
  • Flight Hub for pilots and flight attendants on real trips.
  • Scheduling checklists that re-fire when a trip changes and notify only the item owners.
  • ForeFlight Files: trip sheets and passenger documents pushed automatically.
  • The airport knowledge loop without AI.
  • The same loop for FBOs, hotels, handlers, caterers and individual tails.
  • The expiry engine, with training dates read from myairops.
  • Trips on the crew's Outlook calendars.
  • The daily ops brief, first version, posted to Teams and the wall screens.
  • Joiner, mover and leaver checklists owned by the manager, who assigns subtasks. Tech Log signing authority is removed automatically on the last day, never left as a subtask.
  • The accountability ladder across every module.

Gatesmyairops OData mapping confirmed (OQ2). The trip graph matches myairops on every leg for a month without manual fixes.

An EA books and manages passengers without a phone call, and everyone waiting on a flight knows where it is.

  • FlightAware as the source of live times. Tails are unblocked as needed with Security's agreement, and satcom is the fallback.
  • Emails to passengers, the EA, drivers and the protection team: the 72-hour email, the night-before brief, and block out, taxi, airborne and door-open estimates.
  • Boarded status pulled from the myairops app.
  • Trips on the EA's and principal's Outlook calendars.
  • One-tap passenger feedback after landing.
  • Wall screens for each sub-department on real data.

H3

The 30,000-foot view

Leadership sees the department's state and critical work without asking anyone, and the niche procedures run in-house.

  • Management of change tracked in the lead team's view, starting with the G800 entry into service.
  • The Workday bridge for people, roles and the manager chain, which can also start joiner and leaver checklists.

Designs · 7

Airport knowledge loop

Before a flight, each crew member sees one briefing per airport. Every line says where it came from and how old it is. After the trip, and always after an international one, the crew debriefs in under two minutes. Reviewed debriefs improve the company page, so the next crew's briefing is better than this one.

Company pages are our own 10-7 pages, the operator-specific series in Jeppesen's numbering. We never rebuild the 10-9 airport chart. Production has no airport pages yet, so this is new work for the developer. The sandbox already has source labels, propose, review and publish, per-field confirmation dates and the officer's worklist to start from.

Four tiers of truth

TierExamplesWho can change itHow it shows
OfficialFAA NASR runways and services, NOTAMs and charts through Jeppesen ForeFlight, Airflow's certified dataNobody at GFOSource and cycle date. Never overridden on screen.
CompanyOur 10-7: preferred FBO, handler, customs process, PPR, curfews, ramp limits, crew hotel guidancePropose, review, publish. Safety fields need the chief pilot.The date each field was last confirmed.
Our historyPast GFO trips here, irregularity reports, squawks raised here, typical taxi time, catering issuesDerived from our records“From 9 GFO trips”, with the latest date.
Crew reportDebrief notes not yet reviewedThe reporter, until reviewed“Unreviewed”, with its age and the reporter's role. Hidden after a set age unless confirmed again.
  • A lower tier never overwrites a higher one on screen. A crew report that contradicts official data shows as a conflict and goes to the chief pilot.
  • A crew report becomes company content only through review. Nothing promotes itself.
  • Sensitive fields such as hotels and security arrangements are restricted by default.

New since you last looked

Every airport page and briefing opens with what changed since this pilot last looked at this airport. It is personal, so two pilots on the same trip can see different lists.

  1. Viewing clears ordinary changes. Safety changes need a tap. A change to PPR, a curfew, a ramp limit or a new conflict stays pinned until the pilot taps Got it.
  2. Unacknowledged safety changes follow the pilot to the trip. They appear in Flight Hub readiness. Close to departure, the accountability ladder applies.
  3. A first look is not a diff. It says “First time here” and leads with restrictions.
  4. A long gap gets a summary. “14 changes since March 2025, 2 of them safety”, with the safety ones shown and the full page one tap away.
  5. The marker is the pilot's own. Managers never see who viewed what. Only an unacknowledged safety change before a trip reaches a manager.

The briefing, in order

  1. New since you last looked.
  2. Restrictions and hazards, from official and company tiers only. Always shown.
  3. What our crews said, with ages, and conflicts awaiting review.
  4. Our history at this airport.
  5. The company page, with last-confirmed dates.

The debrief

Required after international trips, first visits, and airports with a stale or conflicted page. Offered otherwise.

  1. “Was everything as briefed?” One tap for yes. That tap confirms every company field the crew was shown, which keeps pages fresh without anyone doing review work.
  2. “What was different?” The briefed fields are listed. The crew taps the wrong ones and types or dictates what they saw.
  3. “Anything new?” Free text or a voice note.
  4. Optional ratings for the FBO, handler and catering.

Review

Debrief items land on the Airport Evaluation Officer's worklist. The officer accepts an item into a company-page proposal, keeps it as a crew report that ages out, or rejects it with a reason the reporter can see. An urgent safety item can publish at once as an airport bulletin, which the page absorbs at its next review. Company pages stay independent of Document Center, so this is built inside the airport module.

Beyond airports

The same tiers and debrief work for FBOs, hotels, handlers and caterers, and for individual tails, where the sandbox's ship notes already hold tail knowledge. Build the loop once and point it at each kind of place or thing crews deal with.

Where Airflow could fit

These are hypotheses for the demos. Airflow describes an open architecture where operators can bring their own agents or tools. What that means in practice is the first thing to learn.

  • Airflow supplies the official tier worldwide, which fills the international gap.
  • Ops Briefing reads our company pages and crew reports, so they reach the crew inside ForeFlight with no second app.
  • Trip Feasibility and our company pages both describe customs, parking, noise and fuel. Where they disagree, the officer hears first.
  • An assistant turns a voice debrief into proposed field changes and spots duplicates. It drafts; the officer decides.

Distribution

  • ForeFlight Documents: a 10-7 style PDF per airport in the pilot content pack.
  • Dispatch, per flight: dispatcher notes or crew notes from operational rules could carry the leg's “what changed”. Confirm which with ForeFlight.
  • Flight Hub: the full interactive briefing, and the only place the debrief happens.

Designs · 8 · New in R3

Disruption options

When something breaks a trip, scheduling should see every realistic option ranked, with the reasoning, instead of building them by phone. Bryan wants Airflow involved. This is a shape that allows it.

What starts it

  • The Tech Log grounds a tail, or a deferral blocks a leg.
  • A crew member becomes unavailable.
  • Weather, a curfew or a NOTAM blocks a leg.
  • The principal's plans change.

How it works

  1. Find what is affected. The sandbox already carries Tech Log status onto the scheduling trip.
  2. Build the options. Wait for the repair using maintenance's estimate, swap to another tail, move the departure, change the routing or add a fuel stop, split the passengers, or use backup lift if GFO has an agreement for it.
  3. Check each option. Feasibility from Airflow, crew duty and fatigue from the crew app, cost from the cost model, and what each passenger misses.
  4. Rank them and show why.
  5. Scheduling decides. myGFO drafts the messages to the EA, crew, passengers, drivers, protection team and maintenance.
  6. Record the decision and the outcome, so the irregularity report and the next disruption both learn from it.

Where Airflow fits

Airflow's Augmented Ops Control does this for airlines today. It ranks possible responses to a disruption and explains its recommendations. It is airline-only so far, so ask whether a business-aviation version is planned. If it is not, our own agent can call Airflow Trip Feasibility once per option through the agent gateway.

First version without AI

Steps 1, 2 and 5 work without AI: the affected trips, the obvious options from the availability engine, and drafted messages. Ranking and feasibility checks arrive with Airflow.

Designs · 9 · New in R3

Agent gateway

Bryan wants myGFO open to AI agents with parts of it blocked, and wants Airflow to connect through the same door. MCP, the Model Context Protocol, is the standard way an AI agent calls outside tools. Copilot Studio supports MCP servers, and ForeFlight's AI Connector is one. This needs much more thought. The shape below is a starting point.

Who asks

  • Copilot in Teams and OutlookEAs, scheduling and managers, under P&G's existing AI governance.
  • Airflow agentsIf Airflow accepts outside tools, it reads operator data from us.
  • myGFO's own featuresDisruption ranking, debrief drafting, regulatory watch.

Through the gateway

  • DocumentsPublished revisions only. Answers cite the section and revision.
  • Fleet statusServiceability, open MEL items, where each tail is.
  • TripsSchedule and legs. Passenger names only for roles that may see them.
  • AirportsCompany pages with their tiers and ages.
  • People and expiriesYour own record. Managers see their team.

Never through the gateway

  • Signing and releasesNo agent signs or returns an aircraft to service.
  • BookingsNo agent commits a trip.
  • Writes to other systemsNo myairops or CAMP writes.
  • Bulk personal dataNo exports of passenger or crew records.

Rules

  • Split by sensitivity. Each server is approved, blocked and audited on its own. A secondary source says Copilot Studio governs whole servers rather than single tools, so the split is what makes blocking possible.
  • On behalf of the person. An agent sees only what the signed-in person can see in myGFO, and every call is logged with both names.
  • Read first. Drafts come later and land in myGFO for a person to approve.
  • Cite everything. An answer names the record, section or revision it came from.

Phases

  1. Documents, read-only, for Copilot. The lowest risk and the first production data set.
  2. Fleet status and trips, read-only, for EAs and scheduling.
  3. Drafts: a trip request, a debrief proposal, disruption messages. A person approves each in myGFO.
  4. Airflow in both directions, once the demos confirm what it supports.

Regulatory change watch

An agent reads FAA changes, compares them with the documents server, and files a suggestion in the document owner's existing queue. It never edits a manual. The owner decides, through the same review every other change goes through.

Designs · 10 · New in R3

SIFL, SEC and the rest

One trip produces several tax and disclosure numbers. Each needs different inputs, and two need actual costs. P&G Tax decides the methods. myGFO's job is to capture the inputs once, correctly, and keep them unchangeable.

CalculationWhat it answersWhat it needs
SIFL imputed incomeThe taxable value of a personal flight for each employee or guestPassenger, purpose per leg, aircraft weight class, statute miles, control-employee status, the SIFL rates for that period
Entertainment disallowanceHow much aircraft cost P&G cannot deduct for entertainment flights by specified individualsPurpose per passenger and leg, occupied seat hours or miles, deadhead legs, actual costs
SEC proxy disclosurePersonal aircraft use by named executive officers, at aggregate incremental costVariable cost per flight: fuel, maintenance per hour, crew travel, landing and parking, catering, handling. Deadhead legs tied to personal trips.
Federal excise taxWhether a charge for a flight is taxable transportationAny money that changes hands, such as time-sharing reimbursements or charges between P&G entities
Time-sharing billingWhat an executive may be charged, if P&G uses time-sharing agreementsThe direct costs 14 CFR 91.501 allows, per flight

Easy to miss

  • SIFL rates change every six months. The engine needs a rate table by period, not one number.
  • SIFL counts statute miles and flight planning uses nautical miles. Convert at the boundary, the same rule as CAMP minutes.
  • The seating-capacity rule can value some personal flights at zero when enough business travelers are aboard. Check who it applies to.
  • The existing tax design already lists control-employee multiples and the security-program provisions.
  • Guests and spouses are attributed to the employee who brought them.
  • Deadhead legs are allocated differently in each calculation.
  • Purpose is captured at booking per passenger and leg, which the booking design already does. A change after the flight is a superseding record, never an edit.
  • Imputed income has to reach payroll for W-2 reporting. Workday may be the path.
  • Each executive needs a year-end statement, and finance needs numbers through the year.
  • The records must be contemporaneous, retained and audit-ready. The append-only ledger helps.
  • The 2017 tax law changed entertainment deductions. P&G Tax decides how the disallowance applies now.

This is a list of what to discuss with P&G Tax. It is not tax advice.

Airflow evaluation

Airflow is the bet that keeps crews in two apps. GFO already uses ForeFlight Dispatch, where Airflow's business-aviation agents live. Bryan's steps are to see the demos, talk to their teams, write the use cases, and then take a recommendation to the VP.

Use cases, in pilot order

  1. Ops briefing with our airport knowledge. Airflow's worldwide data plus our company pages and crew reports, in one ForeFlight briefing.
  2. Trip feasibility for scheduling. “Can a G500 do KLUK to EGGW on Thursday with five passengers?” Customs, fire category, parking, noise, fuel and runway, answered before scheduling commits.
  3. Feasibility inside the EA's request. The EA asks, and the answer comes back with feasibility and cost and a pre-filled request.
  4. Disruption options. Rank the recovery options when a tail goes down. See section 8.
  5. The daily ops brief. Weather and NOTAM impact across the day's flights, posted with fleet status to Teams and the walls.

Questions for the demos

  1. Can Ops Briefing or Trip Feasibility take operator content, such as company pages and crew reports? Through which API?
  2. What does bringing our own agent mean in practice? Can our agent call theirs, and can theirs call our MCP servers?
  3. Can our system call the agents and get structured results back, or do they live only inside Dispatch?
  4. What automation tier does each agent run at? We want advisory only.
  5. How is the reasoning shown, and can we keep it with the trip record?
  6. Does takeoff performance come from approved AFM data for the G500, G650ER and G800?
  7. Is a business-aviation version of Augmented Ops Control planned?
  8. Can it tell us what changed at an international airport since a given date?
  9. Where is our data processed and stored, and is it used for training? What will P&G IT security need?
  10. What does the ForeFlight AI Connector expose, and does it support Copilot or Claude?
  11. What is the pricing model, and when does business aviation get the full rollout?

What the VP pitch needs

One use case shown end to end on a real past trip. The time it saves scheduling or crew, what it replaces, the risk controls, and the cost. Use case 1 is probably the strongest, because the international gap is real and the airport loop is useful even without AI.

  2. Booking

Document control end state

Documents are moving into myGFO. The end state is a document-control system on the level of Comply365, which is the benchmark Bryan named. Comply365's public material describes revision control with automatic syncing, seeing what changed, read and sign in one place, and personal notes kept across updates. The sandbox already has an engine for almost every piece.

CapabilityWhat it meansSandbox engine today
Structured authoringManuals are written as sections and blocks, not uploaded as PDFs.blockEditor.ts, blocks.ts, workbench.ts
Submit a revisionAn author drafts a revision and sends it for review. Readers can send suggestions to the owner.revisions.ts, workbench.ts, suggestions.ts
Compare old and newThe reviewer sees the current and proposed text side by side, with every change marked.diff.ts
Approve inside myGFOFour-eyes approval. The approver can never be the author.The documents reducer
Bulletins absorbedUrgent changes go out at once and fold into the next revision.amendments.ts, bulletinCompat.ts
Read and signEach person signs the revision they read. A new revision asks again.acknowledgments.ts
Notes survive revisionsPersonal highlights and notes carry over to the next revision.annotations.ts
List of effective pagesWhat is current, page by page, for an auditor.effectivePages.ts
Regulatory mappingWhich rule each section satisfies.regCompliance.ts, regCatalog.ts
Word in and outImport an existing Word manual and export one when needed.docxImport.ts, docxExport.ts
Offline and ForeFlightCurrent manuals on the iPad and in ForeFlight Documents.offline.ts. ForeFlight mirroring is not built.

The engines live in src/components/documents/engine/ in the sandbox. Production status is for the developer to confirm.

Appendix · 19 · New in R5

Data loops

Bryan wants data to flow both ways with the outside systems, so each one informs myGFO and myGFO informs it back.

ForeFlight

  • Confirmed. The Dispatch API lets a scheduling system push scheduled flights into Dispatch. Enroute times, performance and flight documents can flow back.
  • Confirmed. Dispatch refuses API updates once a flight is filed, or after someone edits it by hand.
  • Open. Which fields carry actual times, and whether Dispatch sends events when something changes.
  • Open. Whether myairops already pushes flights to Dispatch. If it does, myGFO reads from both instead of pushing flights itself.
  • What myGFO sends. Trips, trip sheets, manuals, airport pages and aircraft status.

Gulfstream

  • Confirmed. On 20 Oct 2026 Gulfstream expanded its Performance Analytics program. It combines aircraft health monitoring, operating data and maintenance history into one view of each aircraft.
  • Confirmed. Gulfstream says its teams addressed nearly 100 potential operational events before they happened this year, and expects to more than double its analytics capabilities in the first half of 2027.
  • Earlier. Gulfstream's health and trend monitoring has reported problems in flight, so a technician and parts could meet the aircraft.
  • Open. What data an operator can receive, in what form, and whether it can reach the Tech Log automatically. Coverage calls this analytics, so confirm any AI claim with Gulfstream.

The knowledge loop

A crew debrief updates the airport page, the page goes to ForeFlight, and the next crew's briefing starts from it. Section 7 has the design.
