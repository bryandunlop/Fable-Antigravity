myGFO Vision and Roadmap

Five departments share one airplane and one trip at a time. myGFO ties them together where their work overlaps, so every fact is entered once and reaches the right people at the right moment.

The visionFive circles, tied together where they overlap The planFive fixes on a glidepath to one end goal Why it mattersWhat changes for everyone, and how we know we have landed

The vision

Five circles. Software lives where they overlap.

Each department is a circle and keeps its own tools. Where two circles meet, work changes hands, and that handoff is where myGFO ties them together.

Show

The end state: every overlap is tied together, with AI answering from the shared record.

Why the overlaps matter

Today a handoff is a phone call. With myGFO it is one record.

Take the busiest overlap, pilots and maintenance. The same pattern repeats at every other overlap on the ring.

One trip

A trip weaves through every circle.

From the EA's first question to the crew's debrief, the trip passes from department to department. myGFO carries it across each handoff.

The plan

The glidepath: five fixes to the end goal.

Each fix lights up more of the circles. We cross a fix only when its gate is met, not on a date. Today we are at the first fix, landing Document Center and the Tech Log.

Outside the circles

How data moves with the systems around us.

Some systems only feed us, like myairops. Others should hear back from us, like ForeFlight. Each loop is drawn only as far as we know it works today.

Who hears what

People hear only what they need, and silence escalates.

Urgent problems find whoever is on call. Overdue work climbs to the manager. Everyone else is left alone.

Why it matters

Everyone who touches GFO gets time back.

Today much of the work at each overlap is chasing, calling and retyping. myGFO gives that time back to the people who fly, fix, plan and travel, and makes the operation safer because nothing depends on someone remembering to pass it on.

Pilots

A defect, a trip detail or an airport note is entered once and is there on the iPad, even offline. Fewer calls, no retyping.

Flight attendants

Trip changes and passenger needs reach you before the trip, not at the aircraft door.

EAs

One place to ask for a trip and see where it stands, without chasing anyone.

Scheduling

Aircraft status and crew readiness arrive as they change, not by text later.

Maintenance

Write-ups arrive complete, repeats are flagged, and nothing is retyped into CAMP.

Passengers

Accurate times and one point of contact, from booking to arrival.

Leadership

One honest view of the operation: what is open, what is late, and who owns it.

How we know we have landed.

The end point is not a date or a feature list. We have arrived when all five of these are true.

  1. One real trip, end to end

    A trip runs from the EA's request to the crew's debrief on production data, and nobody retypes anything.

  2. Every handoff has a home

    Each overlap between two circles has a named owner, and its handoff happens in myGFO, not by phone or text.

  3. No phone call that a system should have made

    People still talk, but never just because a system failed to tell someone.

  4. One place for the record

    Defects, deferrals, releases and signed documents sit in one place and can be audited.

  5. Every gate crossed

    The five fixes are crossed in order, each when its gate is met. Passing the trip test after H4 is touchdown.

After touchdown, the work changes from building toward myGFO to running it and making it better. The decisions and open questions that get us there are in the working reference.
